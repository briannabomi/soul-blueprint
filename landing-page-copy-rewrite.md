# Soul Blueprint — Landing Page Copy Rewrite

**Traffic assumption:** Problem-aware. The person arriving already knows she feels scattered, second-guesses her direction, and has tried enough personal development to suspect the problem isn't more information. She's googling something specific, clicking from Brianna's Instagram or Substack, or was sent this link by someone. She does not need to be convinced she has a problem. She needs to be convinced *this tool* will name it accurately and give her something concrete.

*Why problem-aware:* This is a lead magnet inside an existing brand ecosystem, not a cold-traffic landing page. The visitor has already encountered Brianna's voice somewhere. Leading with the solution, not the problem's existence, respects that.

## HEADLINE CANDIDATES

The current headline "Your Soul Blueprint" fails the six-word test — it names the product, not the outcome. Here are alternatives that lead with what the person gets:

1. **Why Your Soul Incarnated**
2. **Stop Guessing. Your Soul Already Knows.**
3. **What Your Soul Actually Came For**
4. **The Direction Your Mind Can't Find**
5. **Five Maps. One Answer. Yours.**
6. **Not Your Mind's Plan. Your Soul's.**
7. **Your Soul Already Decided. Find Out What.**
8. **What You're Actually Here to Build**

### My pick: **What You're Actually Here to Build**

Why: it passes the six-word / 30-second test — a stranger skimming this page will know exactly what this tool promises. "Actually" does the brand's characteristic work of implying the person has been operating on wrong information. "Here to build" carries conviction and operational specificity — it implies there is something *constructable*, not just something to *feel*. It avoids the word "soul" in the headline (which is everywhere in this space and has lost its stopping power), while the hook line below can immediately reintroduce the soul-as-faculty framing. And it's the kind of sentence someone repeats to a friend: "I did this thing that tells you what you're actually here to build."

**Note for Brianna:** Run the real test. Show three to five people these finalists. Wait 24 hours. Ask which one they remember. The one that sticks wins.

## WHAT I CHANGED / WHAT I DELIBERATELY KEPT

### Changed

**Headline:** `Your Soul Blueprint` → `What You're Actually Here to Build`. The old headline named the product. The new headline names the outcome. A stranger can read this in under two seconds and know what they're getting. "Soul Blueprint" was functioning as a product label, not a promise — it's fine as an internal name and report title, but the welcome screen needs to sell the transformation, not the deliverable.

**Body paragraph 1:** Restructured so the five systems appear *after* the question they answer ("why did your soul incarnate"), not before. The mechanism is now proof of rigor, not the opening bid. The rhetorical question is gone — replaced with a declarative statement that still invites the reader in but doesn't hedge.

**Body paragraph 2:** Split from paragraph 1. Stands alone as a simple logistics line. Removed "to give honest answers" — the Ikigai questions already make that expectation clear, and previewing it here adds a mild barrier. Just tell them what they need.

**CTA button:** `Begin Your Blueprint` → `Find Out`. Shorter, punchier, outcome-oriented. "Begin" implies a process. "Find Out" implies a reveal. The person on this page wants to *know* something — the button should mirror that desire.

**Ikigai 4 subhead:** Removed "AND when you're soul-sourced in your work, money follows." That sentence carried a passive-attraction frame ("money follows") that the editorial translation guide flags as manifestation-adjacent. Replaced with copy that keeps the conviction of "purpose without infrastructure is a journal entry" and extends it: "Your soul didn't come here to have ideas. It came here to build something."

### Deliberately Kept

**The hook line.** `Not what your mind wants. Not what your fear will tolerate. What your soul is actually asking for.` This is the single best piece of copy on the page. It uses the signature "Not X, but Y" device. It names the brand's three-signal model (mind, nervous system, soul) without jargon. It carries total conviction. It's the kind of line someone screenshots and sends to a friend. Don't touch it.

**Ikigai screen subheads (1, 2, 3).** All three are in voice, use the "Not X, but Y" device or concrete narrowing, and do their instructional job without overreaching. They don't need to carry brand weight — the welcome screen and the report do that work.

**Email capture trust box.** "I will never see it. Once you leave the page, it's gone." High-trust, high-conviction, register 3 (operational pragmatism). Exactly right for this surface.

**"Purpose without infrastructure is a journal entry."** This sentence is brand-perfect. Declarative, memorable, impossible to confuse with another coach's voice. It stays as the anchor of the Ikigai 4 screen.

**Birth data screen.** Operational copy. Register 3. Does its job. No brand work needed here — the person is filling in a form, not being sold.
